# Adobe (ADBE) Investment Case

## Business model

Adobe monetizes creative, document, and digital-experience software primarily through recurring subscription revenue. The economic attraction of the model is the combination of high gross margins, recurring revenue, strong cash conversion, and relatively modest physical capital intensity.

FY2025 revenue was $23.769 billion, operating income was $8.706 billion, net income was $7.130 billion, and operating cash flow was $10.031 billion.

## Core earnings drivers

1. Digital Media growth across Creative Cloud, Document Cloud, and AI-enabled products.
2. Digital Experience growth and enterprise customer expansion.
3. Monetization of Firefly and other generative-AI capabilities.
4. Operating-margin progression as revenue scales against R&D and go-to-market investment.
5. Capital allocation, particularly the interaction between repurchases, debt, and per-share earnings.

## Competitive advantages

Adobe benefits from deeply embedded creative workflows, widely used document standards, enterprise distribution, a large installed base, and cross-product integration.

The central analytical question is whether generative AI strengthens those advantages through higher product utility or lowers switching costs by enabling new competitors.

## Variant perception

The model frames the debate around the durability of Adobe's pricing power and AI monetization rather than assuming AI is automatically a tailwind or a threat.

If AI features increase product utility and expand addressable workflows while retention remains high, current R&D investment can support durable free-cash-flow growth. If generative tools commoditize creative workflows faster than Adobe monetizes them, growth and margins can undershoot.

## Catalysts

- Evidence of AI-driven ARR and product adoption
- Better-than-expected Digital Media or Digital Experience growth
- Sustained operating-margin expansion
- Capital-return discipline and share-count reduction

## Downside risks

- Faster competitive disruption from AI-native products
- Slower enterprise demand
- Pricing pressure or higher acquisition costs
- Execution risk in acquisitions
- Regulatory, IP, and content-rights issues around generative AI
- Aggressive repurchases at unattractive valuations

## Valuation framework

The project uses a five-year FCFF DCF with explicit bull, base, and bear assumptions for revenue growth, operating margin, WACC, terminal growth, CapEx, working capital, debt changes, and share-count evolution.

Validated DCF outputs:

- Bear: **$275.90/share**
- Base: **$447.77/share**
- Bull: **$670.76/share**

These values are scenario outputs, not price targets or recommendations. They are sensitive to growth, margin, WACC, and terminal-growth assumptions.

The DCF divides current equity value by FY2025 diluted shares of 427 million. Future share-count reductions are not used to boost present per-share value without explicitly accounting for the cash cost of repurchases.

## Interpretation

The case study is intended to demonstrate a defensible public-equity research process: reported historicals, explicit forecast assumptions, scenario analysis, cash-flow valuation, sensitivity analysis, and documented risks.

Trading-comparable multiples are implemented as a refreshable framework and should be updated separately when a current market snapshot is required.
