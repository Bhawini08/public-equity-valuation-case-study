# Adobe (ADBE) Investment Case

## Business model

Adobe monetizes creative, document, and digital-experience software primarily through recurring subscription revenue. The economic attraction of the model is the combination of high gross margins, large recurring revenue, strong cash conversion, and relatively modest physical capital intensity.

FY2025 revenue was $23.769 billion, with subscription revenue representing the large majority of sales. Operating income was $8.706 billion and operating cash flow was $10.031 billion.

## Core earnings drivers

1. Digital Media growth, including Creative Cloud, Document Cloud, and AI-enabled products.
2. Digital Experience growth and enterprise customer expansion.
3. Monetization of Firefly and other generative-AI capabilities.
4. Operating-margin progression as revenue scales against R&D and go-to-market investment.
5. Capital allocation, particularly the interaction between large repurchases, debt issuance, and per-share earnings.

## Competitive advantages

Adobe benefits from deeply embedded creative workflows, widely used document standards, enterprise distribution, a large installed base, and cross-product integration. The key analytical question is whether AI strengthens those advantages or lowers switching costs by enabling new competitors.

## Variant perception

The model frames the debate around the durability of Adobe's pricing power and AI monetization rather than assuming that AI is automatically either a tailwind or a threat. If AI features increase product utility and expand addressable workflows while Adobe maintains high retention, current R&D investment can support durable free-cash-flow growth. If generative tools commoditize creative workflows faster than Adobe monetizes them, growth and margins can undershoot.

## Catalysts

- Evidence of AI-driven ARR and product adoption
- Better-than-expected Digital Media or Digital Experience growth
- Sustained margin expansion
- Capital-return discipline and share-count reduction

## Downside risks

- Faster competitive disruption from AI-native products
- Slower enterprise demand
- Pricing pressure or higher customer acquisition costs
- Execution risk in acquisitions
- Regulatory, IP, and content-rights issues around generative AI
- Aggressive repurchases at unattractive valuations

## Valuation

The project uses a five-year FCFF DCF with explicit bull, base, and bear assumptions for growth, operating margin, WACC, terminal growth, CapEx, working capital, and share count. Trading-comparable multiples are implemented as a refreshable framework rather than frozen market data.

No investment recommendation is embedded in the code. The final market-price comparison and peer-multiple refresh should be completed with current data during the live validation pass.
